## 1.What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?

The scientific name of the chosen plant is *Zea mays*. The plastid genome accession number is **NC_001666.2**.

## 2. What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?

The total length of the *Zea mays* chloroplast genome is **140,384 base pairs (bp)**.

## 3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions

The plastid genome contains four main regions: the **Large Single-Copy (LSC)** region, **Small Single-Copy (SSC)** region, and two **Inverted Repeat (IR)** regions, **IRa** and **IRb**. In the map, the LSC is the largest region, while the SSC is the smaller region at the bottom. IRa and IRb are the two duplicated regions located on opposite sides of the map.

## 4. Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region

Three examples of genes located in the LSC region are:

- *psbA* – involved in Photosystem II
  
- *rbcL* – codes for the large subunit of Rubisco
  
- *atpA* – codes for a subunit of ATP synthase


## 5. Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region

One example of a gene located in the SSC region is *ndhF*. It is associated with the NADH dehydrogenase complex and is involved in photosynthetic electron transport.

## 6. ive at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the geneshownmore than once because of the duplicated IR regions?

One example of a gene located in an inverted repeat region is *rrn16*. Because the IR regions are duplicated, genes located within these regions can also occur twice in the chloroplast genome. Therefore, the gene can appear in both IRa and IRb.

## 7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.

The *psbA* gene is a photosynthesis-related gene found on the map. It codes for the D1 protein of Photosystem II, which is important for the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis.

## 8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?

The arrows show the direction in which the genes are transcribed. Genes pointing in opposite directions are transcribed from different DNA strands. This shows the orientation of genes within the chloroplast genome.

## 9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.

The GC content does not appear completely uniform across the entire plastid genome. The graph shows some variation in GC content among different regions of the genome. This indicates that the proportion of G and C bases changes across the genome.

## 10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the rawDNAsequence or a long list of annotations?

A graphical plastid genome map makes the genome easier to understand compared with viewing only a raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations. It allows the locations of genes, LSC, SSC, and IR regions, gene orientation, and GC content to be viewed more easily in one figure.
